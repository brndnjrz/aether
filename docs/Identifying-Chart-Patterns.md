# Identifying Chart Patterns with Technical Analysis

**Source:** Fidelity Investments — Technical Analysis Webinar Series
**Coauthor:** Charles D. Kirkpatrick II, CMT — President of Kirkpatrick & Company, Inc.

---

## Understanding Patterns and Their Limits

### Defining Patterns

- A pattern is bounded by at least two trend lines (straight or curved)
- All patterns have a combination of entry and exit points
- Patterns can be **continuation patterns** or **reversal patterns**
- Patterns are **fractal** — they appear in any charting period (weekly, daily, minute, etc.)
- A pattern is NOT complete or activated until an actual breakout occurs

### The Limits of Patterns

Some human tendencies that are dangerous for investors:

- See patterns where there aren't any
- Believe "market lore" (technical or fundamental) without evidence
- Look backwards rather than forward
- Stick with original price targets after conditions have changed

## Techniques for Trading Patterns

### Breakouts

A breakout is a violation of a trend line, support/resistance, or previous reversal point. It signifies a change in buyer and seller behavior and signals the beginning or end of a trend.

### Confirmation Filters

Apply a confirmation filter to determine whether a breakout has taken place:

- **Intrabar** — price action within the bar
- **Multiple closes** — several closes beyond the level
- **Time** — stays beyond the level for X periods
- **Percentage or point** — moves a minimum distance
- **Money** — dollar amount beyond the level

### Entry Stops

Buy stop orders are used to enter trades once the price breaks out. Placed above resistance breakouts or below trend line breakouts.

### False and Failed Breakouts

- **False Breakout** — Price breaks out but almost immediately returns back through the breakout price
- **Failed Breakout (Trap)** — A false breakout occurs and the price then breaks out in the opposite direction

### Protective Stops

- Protects capital by determining the amount of risk before entry
- Placement types:
  - Filters (percent, points, or money)
  - Trend line, support or resistance level with filter

### Trading False Breakouts Using Protective Stops

1. Enter on breakout
2. Place protective stop outside breakout bar (opposite from breakout direction)
3. Place entry stop at same level ("stop and reverse" order)
4. If price continues in breakout direction — profit from breakout entry
5. If breakout is false — profit from stop and reverse

### Retracements

Counter-trend corrections after a breakout:

- **Pullback** — correction on a breakout down
- **Throwback** — correction on a breakout up

Note: Retracements don't always occur, and performance can suffer when they do.

## Horizontal Congestion Patterns

### Double Top

- Two successive peaks separated by an opposite reversal point
- Peaks are roughly the same price (resistance level) — either rounded or pointed
- Pattern completes on breakout below the middle reversal point
- **Target:** Height from highest peak to trough, subtracted from breakout price

### Double Bottom

- Two successive troughs separated by a peak
- Troughs are roughly the same price (support level)
- Pattern completes on breakout above the middle peak
- **Target:** Distance from troughs to peak, added to breakout price

### Triple Top

- Three distinct peaks at roughly the same price, separated by two intermittent troughs
- Breakout occurs when price exceeds the extreme of the intermittent trough
- **Target:** Height from highest peak to lowest trough, subtracted from the lowest trough

### Triple Bottom

- Three distinct troughs at roughly the same price, separated by two intermittent peaks
- Best performance may be after a sustained decline
- Average performance — watch for failures
- **Target:** Height from highest peak to lowest trough, added to the highest peak

### Rectangles

- Trading range bounded by support and resistance levels
- Slight tilt, similar to horizontal channel
- Often has many false breakouts — confirm breakouts carefully
- "Shortfall" is often an indicator of eventual breakout direction
- Best occurrence may be bottom breaking upward
- **Target:** Height of the rectangle (resistance to support), added to resistance (upside) or subtracted from support (downside)

## Triangle Patterns

### Symmetrical Triangle

- Bounded by a downward-sloping upper trend line and upward-sloping lower trend line
- Prices must touch each bound at least twice
- Many false breakouts; moderately successful performance
- Best occurrence may be upward breakout (above average for all patterns)
- **Target:** Height from highest peak to lowest trough, added/subtracted from breakout price

### Ascending Triangle

- Horizontal upper trend line + upward-sloping lower trend line
- Breaks more commonly upward
- Breakout usually occurs within pattern; many small false breakouts
- Post-breakout performance: average on upside, above average on downside
- **Target:** Same as symmetrical triangle

### Descending Triangle

- Horizontal lower trend line + downward-sloping upper trend line
- Breaks most commonly downward
- Above-average performance on upside break; retracements occur often
- **Target:** Same as symmetrical triangle

### Wedge

- Two trend lines headed in the same direction
- Price must touch a trend line at least five times (3 on one, 2 on the other) before breakout
- Often occur after a panic (declining wedge) or bubble (rising wedge)
- Performance is below average; retracements are very common
- **Target (downward breakout):** Lowest trough in the pattern
- **Target (upward breakout):** Height of pattern added to breakout price

## Other Multi-Bar Patterns

### Head and Shoulders: Top

- Three peaks — center peak (head) higher than the other two (shoulders)
- Shoulders at approximately the same level
- "Neckline" connects the two troughs between the peaks
- Pattern only complete on breaking the neckline
- One of the lowest failure rates of all patterns
- **Target:** Distance from head to neckline, projected downward from neckline

### Head and Shoulders: Bottom (Inverse)

- Inverted version of the top pattern
- Otherwise identical except not as profitable
- **Target:** Distance from head to neckline, projected upward from neckline

### Cup and Handle (Saucer)

- Rounded bottom (NOT a "V" bottom), two "lips" at each end, and a "handle" (flag-like)
- Pattern completes on breakout above both lips
- Often has a throwback
- Performance ranks about average for bottom patterns
- **Target:** Height of right cup lip to bottom of cup, added to breakout price

## Candlestick Patterns

### General Rules

- Traditionally reversal patterns — used to identify when a trend is ending
- Best performance: upward reversal patterns after a correction in a longer upward trend (and vice versa)
- Do NOT act until the pattern has formed and is activated by a breakout

### Doji

- One-candle pattern: open and close are the same price, high and low roughly equidistant
- Extremely common
- Indicates indecision — possible warning of price change

### Harami

- Two-candle pattern: large body followed by small body of opposite color
- Second body is completely within the first body ("spinning top")
- Can break either way despite common belief as a reversal pattern

### Hanging Man and Hammer

- One-candle patterns differentiated by body color
- High coincides with either opening or closing price
- **Hanging Man** — thought to be continuation, actually breaks randomly with slight upward bias; below-average performance
- **Hammer** — occurs relatively frequently but below-average performance

### Shooting Star and Inverted Hammer

- One-candle inverted hanging man/hammer patterns
- Hammers have white bodies; shooting stars have black bodies
- Both have average performance as single-candle patterns

### Engulfing

- Two-bar pattern: second bar body completely engulfs the first bar body
- Bottom engulfing (short black body followed by tall white body) — very good performance on downward breakout in a downward trend

### Dark Cloud Cover and Piercing Line

- **Dark Cloud Cover** — second bar closes higher than the first; black vs. white; downward reversing pattern
- **Piercing Line** — opposite; second bar is white and lower than the black first bar; upward reversing pattern

## Short-Term Patterns

### Pennant / Flag

- Preceded by a steep, sharp price change (up or down)
- Forms a short consolidation resembling a triangle (pennant) or parallelogram (flag)
- Generally slopes slightly opposite to the trend
- Breakout often followed by a move equal to the earlier sharp price change
- **Target:** Height of the "flagpole" added to bottom of the pennant/flag

### Gaps

- No trading at specific prices between one close and the following open
- Caused by appreciable changes in supply/demand
- Generally profitable on breakouts from patterns, trends, support/resistance

**Explosion Gap Pivot method:**

1. After the gap, wait for throwback
2. If throwback covers the gap — no action
3. If throwback stops — this is the "pivot low"
4. Place buy entry above the high of the gap bar
5. Protective stops initially at gap low, then below pivot low

### Pipe Bottom (Two-Bar Reversal)

- Two bars at the end of a large trend
- First bar closes at the low; second bar closes in upper half of range
- Bar ranges are larger than preceding bar ranges
- More reliable in weekly data
- Action occurs on breakout through second bar
- **Target:** Height from taller bar to lower bar, added to taller bar

### Volatility Patterns (Narrow Range / Inside Bar)

- New trends often begin from periods of low volatility
- **Inside Bar** — a bar whose range is completely within the preceding bar's range
  - Buy: next bar opening above inside bar close
  - Sell: next bar opening below inside bar close
- **Narrow Range (NR4)** — fourth bar has a narrower range than the preceding three
  - Breakout occurs on a break above or below the narrow range bar's high/low
